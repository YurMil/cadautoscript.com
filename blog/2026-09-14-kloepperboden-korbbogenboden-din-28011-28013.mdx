Dished ends close almost every cylindrical pressure vessel. In German-speaking
markets two shapes are standard: the **torispherical (Klöpper) head** per
DIN 28011 and the **korbbogen head** per DIN 28013. They look alike at first
glance but differ clearly in height, material and load-carrying behaviour. This
post explains the geometry, works through an example, and shows where geometry
ends and the strength calculation begins.

## Anatomy of a dished end

A head per DIN 28011 or DIN 28013 has three regions:

1. **Crown** – the central spherical cap with the **crown radius R**.
2. **Knuckle** – the toroidal transition with the **knuckle radius r**.
3. **Straight flange** – the cylindrical portion of **height h₁** that is welded
   to the shell.

All dimensions refer to the **outside diameter Dₐ**. Calculating from the inside
diameter by mistake produces a head that does not fit the shell.

## The formulas

| Quantity | Torispherical (DIN 28011) | Korbbogen (DIN 28013) |
|---|---|---|
| Crown radius R | `R = Dₐ` | `R = 0.8 · Dₐ` |
| Knuckle radius r | `r = 0.1 · Dₐ` | `r = 0.154 · Dₐ` |
| Inside dish depth h₂ | `h₂ = 0.1935 · Dₐ − 0.455 · s` | `h₂ = 0.255 · Dₐ − 0.635 · s` |
| Usual straight flange h₁ | `h₁ ≥ 3.5 · s` | `h₁ ≥ 3 · s` |

The **overall height** is straight flange plus inside dish depth plus wall
thickness:

```
H = h₁ + h₂ + s
```

The korbbogen head has a smaller crown radius and a much larger knuckle. It is
therefore **taller** and needs **more plate** – but it distributes the stresses
in the knuckle more favourably. That is why it is preferred for higher pressures
or wall thicknesses. The flatter torispherical head is the economical choice for
lower pressures and where headroom is limited.

## Worked example: Dₐ = 1000 mm, s = 10 mm

**Torispherical head per DIN 28011**

```
R  = 1000 mm
r  = 0.1 · 1000                 = 100 mm
h₂ = 0.1935 · 1000 − 0.455 · 10 = 188.95 mm
h₁ = 3.5 · 10                   = 35 mm
H  = 35 + 188.95 + 10           = 233.95 mm
```

**Korbbogen head per DIN 28013**

```
R  = 0.8 · 1000                 = 800 mm
r  = 0.154 · 1000               = 154 mm
h₂ = 0.255 · 1000 − 0.635 · 10  = 248.65 mm
h₁ = 3 · 10                     = 30 mm
H  = 30 + 248.65 + 10           = 288.65 mm
```

At the same diameter and wall thickness the korbbogen head is about
**55 mm taller**.

## Blank size and weight

The head is spun or pressed from a circular blank. For a first estimate of the
blank diameter the calculator uses the approximations common in practice:

```
Torispherical:  D_blank ≈ 1.09 · Dₐ + 2 · h₁
Korbbogen:      D_blank ≈ 1.14 · Dₐ + 2 · h₁
```

For the example in steel (7.85 kg/dm³):

| | Torispherical | Korbbogen |
|---|---|---|
| Blank diameter | 1.09 · 1000 + 70 = **1160 mm** | 1.14 · 1000 + 60 = **1200 mm** |
| Blank weight | ≈ **83.0 kg** | ≈ **88.8 kg** |

This is an **estimate for quoting and material planning**, not the final cutting
size. Forming method, trim allowance and thinning in the knuckle are set by the
head manufacturer. The calculator therefore also reports an estimated minimum
wall thickness after forming – an indication, not proof.

## Where geometry ends

DIN 28011 and DIN 28013 define the **shape**. Whether a head withstands the
pressure is a separate question:

- In Europe, dished ends under internal pressure are verified per
  **EN 13445-3, Clause 7**.
- In Germany, **AD 2000-Merkblatt B3** (dished ends under internal and external
  pressure) is also widely used.

Both account for the highest stresses occurring in the **knuckle** – exactly
where the material flows most during forming. The nominal thickness from the
geometry must therefore always be checked against the **minimum thickness after
forming** and the **corrosion allowance**.

## Common mistakes

- **Inside instead of outside diameter.** All formulas use Dₐ.
- **Forgetting the straight flange.** Without enough straight flange the weld
  ends up in the knuckle – exactly where it does not belong.
- **Taking the blank weight as the finished weight.** Trim is lost; the finished
  head is lighter.
- **Thickening a torispherical head instead of choosing a korbbogen head.** More
  wall helps less in the knuckle than a more favourable shape.
